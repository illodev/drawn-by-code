# red-thread · «El hilo rojo»

- **Style:** sumi-e (new: Japanese ink painting on washi, vermilion as the only colour)
- **Format:** 16:9 · 68 s · 24 fps (style test: 7 s, shot 7; animatic v1: the whole film)
- **Purpose / audience:** a short film for drawn-by-code that touches the heart: a parable
  about today's society, broken by a lack of humanity and by growing polarization.
- **One-line idea:** in a river village everyone is tied to everyone by an invisible red
  thread (the Japanese *akai ito*); screens and slogans split the village into two banks,
  the threads snap and the red drains out of the world, until a child's red kite carries one
  thread back across the river.
- **References:** the user's four images (2026-11-04): a plum branch with a hanging cage and
  a roof on embossed paper; a boatman under a red sun in grey dry brush; a cherry tree, cranes
  and a torii in black and red; a red pavilion and bridge in rain. Taken from them: textured
  paper, black ink in many greys, red only for the sun, blossoms and what matters, tiny ink
  silhouettes for people, water as horizontal dry-brush strokes with reflections.
- **On-screen text (literal, Spanish):** title «El hilo rojo» with a seal 赤い糸; closing line
  «Antes de ser bandos, fuimos vecinos.» (approved by the user, 2026-11-04). Latin text in
  Kaushan Script (a brush script, OFL); the seal's kanji in Yuji Syuku (OFL, subset).
- **Music / rhythm (round 14):** Japanese lo-fi, one ElevenLabs piece per scene in D with its
  own rhythm, crossfaded at the cuts (round 12 was traditional instruments only, crossfaded at the cuts (see review.md). Before that: synthesized, licence-free: a koto (plucked string) in the Japanese
  *in* scale, a breathy flute (shakuhachi-like), wind and water; 60 BPM, cuts on whole seconds.
  The dark middle loses the melody (a drone, a low drum); the melody returns, warmer, when the
  red comes back.
- **Must NOT happen:** preaching with words (the story is told by pictures); a side being
  «the good one» (both banks lose their red, both give it back); colours other than ink and
  vermilion (the screens are white cuts in the ink, not blue); cartoon faces.

## The cause (rounds 11–12)

The kuroko, the black-clad stagehand of Japanese theatre whom the audience agrees not to
see, stands for the system that divides people without their knowing. He is revealed in
steps (round 12): first only his sleeve, dropping news broadsheets (kawaraban, the Edo
period's own medium: phones were out of period) into everyone's hands; then one sheet in
close-up, headed 対岸に鬼あり («there are demons on the other bank»), whose picture turns the
neighbour into an oni while we read; when it blows away everyone wears the mask. Only when
the shouting starts does the camera pull back and find him whole, a string from his control
crosses to every sheet. Nobody looks up. When people let the sheets go his strings break and
he dissolves into the mist.

## Round 14

No red threads at all: the greetings are bows, and red is the sun, the blossoms and the
children's kite string (from which the bridge is woven).

## The evolving idea

**Red is humanity.** At the start the red is everywhere: the sun, the cherry blossoms and the
thin red threads between people who greet each other. As people stop looking at each other,
the threads go slack and snap, the blossoms turn grey, the sun drains. The last red thing in
the world is a child's kite. Its string becomes the thread across the river, and the red
flows back along it into everyone who takes hold of it.

## Characters (locked in the style test)

- **The girl:** small ink silhouette, bob haircut with a short tail tied with red, a short
  kimono-like jacket over trousers; holds the kite string in her right hand.
- **The boy:** small ink silhouette, short spiky hair, a hooded jacket; holds a phone (a white
  cut in the ink) until he looks up.
- **Villagers:** ink silhouettes in a few strokes (straw hats, robes, coats), no faces: a
  face would be a cartoon. Who they are reads from posture.
- **Hands:** at the figures' size (120–180 units tall) a hand is one ink dab at the end of the
  sleeve, as in the references (the boatman's hands are not drawn). Any close-up of a hand
  goes through the **hands** skill first.
