# physics-newton-faraday

- **Style:** risograph
- **Format:** 16:9 · 12 s · 24 fps (drawings on twos)
- **Purpose / audience:** the decisive test of a 40 s short, «A history of physics in 40
  seconds». These 12 s are its shots 3 and 4 (8–20 s of the full piece). The test passes
  if the fall, the orbit and the induction read **without any text**.
- **One-line idea:** a dropped ball's fall becomes an orbit, the orbit winds into a coil,
  and a magnet moved through that coil makes a needle answer.
- **References:** portraits of Newton (long hair, dark coat, white neckcloth) and Faraday
  (thick hair at the sides, side-parted, dark suit, high collar and stock). Physics
  sources, which stay out of the video:
  - NASA Goddard, [Newton's law of universal gravitation](https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/features/yba/CygX1_mass/gravity/more.html):
    the fall near the ground and the orbit are the same gravity.
  - Royal Institution, [Faraday's generator](https://www.rigb.org/explore-science/explore/collection/michael-faradays-generator):
    magnet, coil and galvanometer. The needle answers the *change* of flux.
- **On-screen text (literal):** none. No names, dates, formulas or captions.
- **Music / rhythm:** 120 BPM. Each 6 s shot is three bars. Sound design, to be made
  later: a dry knock, then a plucked-string arc for the orbit. For the magnet, two
  pulses of different timbre, going in and coming out, with a short silence while it
  is held.
- **Must NOT happen:**
  - an apple on the head;
  - orbits drawn as wires that hold bodies up;
  - the planet born out of the ball: the table becomes a conceptual model;
  - a lamp or needle that stays lit or deflected while the magnet is still;
  - field lines read as currents crossing the air;
  - a fifth ink.

## Shot list (scene time; full-piece time in brackets)

| Time | Shot | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.0 (8–9) | Newton · hold | Newton, in the left third, holds a brass ball between his right thumb and index finger over the table and looks at it. |
| 1.0–1.5 (9–9.5) | Newton · drop | He lets go: the ball leaves his fingers and falls a short way to the table, leaving an amber trace. Dry knock. |
| 1.5–3.0 (9.5–11) | Newton · model | The camera pulls back. The table's edge under the ball stretches and curves until it closes into a schematic Earth, with the ball resting on top. The table under Newton stays wood; the rest turns into a model. |
| 3.0–5.0 (11–13) | Newton · orbit | A second object leaves sideways from above the ball. Its path curves all the way round the Earth. The fall and the orbit read together, with the Earth as their common centre (faint blue lines to the centre). Newton's eyes follow both. |
| 5.0–6.0 (13–14) | Newton · wind | The orbit trace thickens into copper, tilts and winds into a helix: the coil. |
| 6.0–6.8 (14–14.8) | Faraday · bench | The helix lies on a bench as a copper coil wired to a galvanometer on the right. Faraday is on the left, a bar magnet in his right hand. |
| 6.8–8.0 (14.8–16) | Faraday · in | He pushes the magnet into the coil and the needle swings right. |
| 8.0–8.8 (16–16.8) | Faraday · hold | The magnet is held still and the needle goes back to zero. |
| 8.8–10.0 (16.8–18) | Faraday · out | He pulls the magnet out and the needle swings left. |
| 10.0–12.0 (18–20) | Faraday · spot | The camera pushes into the dial. The needle's tip becomes a light spot that keeps sliding along a scale: the link to the next laboratory. |

Checks from the script:
- at 7.5 s the needle answers;
- at 8.5 s, with the magnet still, it is back towards zero;
- at 9.5 s it answers the other way.
