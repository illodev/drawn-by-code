# physics-history

> **v2 (user, round 2):** the script below is replaced by the chain in «Script v2» at the
> end. The v1 table stays as history.

- **Style:** risograph (four inks on a night-blue ground; see `.claude/skills/style-risograph`)
- **Format:** 16:9 · 40 s · 24 fps (960 frames, drawings on twos) · 1920 × 1080, composed so a
  4K render stays possible
- **Purpose / audience:** a short film for the public repo: how far code-drawn animation can
  go when it has to explain something true.
- **One-line idea:** one gaze crosses six ways of understanding the world. An observation
  becomes a path, the path becomes an instrument, the instrument reveals something invisible,
  space changes, and matter stops behaving like little balls.
- **Emotion:** curiosity, then understanding by association, then wonder.
- **On-screen text (literal):** none. No names, dates, formulas or captions. The sources and
  credits go in the publication text, outside the 40 s.
- **Music / rhythm:** 120 BPM. The opening and the close are one bar each; every scene is three
  bars. A motif of few notes gains an instrument per stage: glass, plucked string, brass,
  granular texture, sustained string, harmonics. Audio comes in a later round.
- **Continuity:**
  - An amber guide line leads the eye. It changes function between scenes through graphic
    transitions.
  - It is never the same particle travelling between phenomena. No physical equivalence is
    suggested between orbits, currents, radiation and wave functions.
- **Must NOT happen:**
  - an apple on Newton's head;
  - orbits drawn as wires;
  - a lamp or needle that stays lit or deflected with the magnet still;
  - radiation as a glowing liquid;
  - a modern Geiger counter;
  - the rubber-sheet funnel with an outside «down»;
  - melting clocks, E=mc², black holes and gravitational waves in the same six seconds;
  - an electron as a ball orbiting the cloud;
  - Schrödinger's cat;
  - white-haired old Einstein in a 1910s scene;
  - Marie Curie credited alone with discovering radioactivity (Becquerel came first);
  - a fifth ink.

## The six figures

| Figure | What we show | What must stay accurate |
|---|---|---|
| Galileo | Telescope, Jupiter's disc and four small points that change position (1610) | The observation is condensed from several nights; he did not invent the telescope |
| Newton | A fall near a surface and an orbit round the same body | The fall on Earth and the orbit are the same gravity |
| Faraday | Magnet, coil, galvanometer | The needle answers the *change* of flux, not a still magnet |
| Marie Curie | Mineral samples, an ionisation chamber and electrometer, an abstract layer of emission | Measurement of radioactivity; discoveries shared with Pierre; Becquerel first |
| Einstein | A 3D lattice, a mass, light paths bent more near it than far | The lattice is a representation, not a sheet or a funnel |
| Schrödinger | A confined wave with stable nodes, then its spatial intensity | Density is \|ψ\|² (Born's interpretation); nodes stay put; phase may turn, density does not pulse |

Faces follow period portraits:
- **Galileo, 1610:** short dark beard, moustache, high forehead, a light collar.
- **Newton:** long wavy hair and a dark coat, as in the test.
- **Faraday:** thick side hair, a suit, a high collar and stock, as in the test.
- **Marie Curie, c. 1900:** hair up, a high-necked dark dress.
- **Einstein, 1910s:** dark tousled hair, a moustache, a suit.
- **Schrödinger, c. 1926:** round glasses, combed hair, a suit.

## Script v2 (approved by the user after round 2)

The piece is one continuous chain of transformations with ironic humour. Nobody sits. The
figures are full body, upright and moving. Framing is close, the camera travels, and every
join is one object turning into the next.

| Time | Scene |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Galileo** on a rooftop at night, a close profile at the eyepiece. The camera travels along the tube to the sky; nights pass fast and Jupiter's moons move. The zoom goes on until Jupiter fills the frame and its bands turn into the skin of an **apple** (a stalk and a leaf appear). |
| 5–12 | **Newton** lies under the tree by day. The apple falls, the camera follows it, and it lands on his head (bonk, little stars circling). He gets up, picks it up, looks at it, glances at the daytime Moon and throws it up hard. The camera pulls away, Powers of Ten style: the field, the hills, the coast, the curve of the Earth. The apple rises and bends into an orbit round the globe. |
| 12–18 | The orbit's trails spiral into Faraday's **coil**. A psychedelic zoom through copper rings and blooming field lines lands in his lab, close and low-angled: Faraday, standing, thrusts the magnet in and out, the needle kicks and a spark jumps. |
| 18–24 | The spark becomes the blue-green glow of a **radium** tube (its salts really glowed). Marie Curie lifts it to her face in the dark shed while the camera circles her. Particles fly out in slow motion towards us and one stretches into a ray of light. |
| 24–30 | Young **Einstein** runs beside the ray: his thought experiment of chasing light. The spacetime grid streams past, bends round a huge Sun, and the ray bends with it. The grid folds into a **cube**. |
| 30–40 | The cube becomes a wooden **box**. The lid opens and **Schrödinger and the cat** appear. The frame splits into 2, 4, 8, 16…, each cell a different outcome: the cat dead and Schrödinger alive, the reverse, both dancing, the cat shutting the lid on him, the cat playing chess… until a mosaic of probability. Last beat: every cat looks at the camera at once. |
