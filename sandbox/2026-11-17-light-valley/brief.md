# light-valley

- **Style:** woodcut (new: a relief print cut in a plank, one black key block and a second
  block in ochre that prints only the electric light)
- **Format:** 16:9 · the film 75 s (25 bars of 4/4 at 80 BPM: a beat is 0.75 s, a bar 3 s) · 24 fps, drawings on twos · the style test 6 s
- **Purpose / audience:** the user asked for «serious and solid styles for a new video».
  Eight candidates were shown as a board of generated images; the user chose «the woodcut,
  but not a lighthouse», then the subject «The light comes to the valley» out of three
  proposals, and left the rest to us: «Decide for yourself; the only thing is, don't
  generate any more images with ElevenLabs.»
- **One-line idea:** a mountain valley without electricity; pole by pole the line comes
  down the slope, and every light that comes on is carved out of the black block and
  printed in ochre, until the whole valley is cut.
- **The device:** in a woodcut the light is what is taken away. The film's light is
  literally carved: a lamp or a window that comes on is the gouge going into the block over
  two or three drawings, and the ochre block (the only colour) prints only electric light.
  Before the line arrives there is only black on paper.
- **References** (generated for this film with ElevenLabs, gpt-image-2, by us; in `ref/`):
  - `street-lit.png`: the night the light arrives, the village street with its lamps, the
    family at a lit door (matched in the style test).
  - `valley-night.png`: the valley before electricity (its cut is finer than the one
    chosen for the film).
  - `raising-pole.png`: the line crew raising a pole on the slope, a mule with the cable
    drum: the coarse cut chosen for the whole film.
  - `variants-*.jpg`: the four variants of each, as they were generated.
- **Decisions taken for the user:** the coarse cut of `raising-pole.png` for every shot
  (it is woodcut, not wood engraving, it reads at speed and after compression); the ochre
  block only for electric light; the moon goes from a crescent to full over the film (the
  weeks the line takes).
- **On-screen text (literal):** «CORRIENTE», carved at the foot of the last view of the valley (and seen mirrored on the block when the sheet is peeled off). Nothing else. «Corriente» is both the river's current and the electric one: the film's idea in one word.
- **Music / rhythm:** 80 BPM, sober (a cello and a low piano, or a classical guitar), cuts on the bar or the beat. One sound effect only: the gouge going into the wood, every time something is carved (the river's first streak, each light), and at the end the sheet peeled off the block. To be confirmed with the user (the source: ElevenLabs music and sound effects, if allowed).
- **Must NOT happen:** a light that glows instead of being carved; any colour but ochre
  (and the ochre never on anything the electric light does not touch); fine engraved
  hatching (that is the engraving style); lens-shaped «eyes» for stones; ovals of flat
  ochre for pools of light; a lighthouse.

## The film: «Corriente» (script)

Three structures were crossed and the best of each kept: the wire's journey from the power
house to a bulb (the through-line), the same valley on several nights while the moon waxes
(the clock and the repetition), and an old woman's nightly oil lamp (the human beat).

**Rules of the film**
- Light is carved. Candle and oil light are paper white (a nick, a teardrop); electric light
  is carved and printed in ochre. The ochre appears for the first time at 0:39, when the switch
  is thrown, and never before.
- The wire is the through-line: from 0:18 on, a single carved white line runs through every
  shot, and it is always the same line (the same drum, the same poles, the same street).
- The river's current turns into the electric one: the film opens on the river's first cut
  and the power house is the river's own.
- The moon is the clock: a crescent at the start, half, gibbous, full on the night of the
  light (the weeks the line takes).
- Night shots are almost all black; day shots invert (mostly paper, black lines and dashes),
  in the coarse cut of `ref/raising-pole.png`.
- The camera pans and tilts across the plank, never zooms into it (the cuts would scale);
  only in the last shot is the print handled as a sheet.

**What the production needs** (decided before animating): the sets (the valley at night and
by day, the gorge and power house, the switchback slope, the street, the old woman's room and
window, the bench with the block); the cast drawn from `ref/raising-pole.png`'s build (the
line crew, the lineman, the muleteer and the mule), the old woman and the family; hands that
strike a match, haul a rope, throw a lever and cup a flame, each from a photographic
reference and a hand check sheet the user approves (skill **hands**); the sheet peeled off
the block (`Motion.quad` with a bend), and the block itself printed as wood (carved areas pale
grained wood, uncut areas shiny ink).
